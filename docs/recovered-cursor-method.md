# Recovered iterative research method

## Provenance and evidentiary status

This design input was supplied by the project owner on 2026-09-03 as a prose
synthesis titled *Reusable Iterative Research Method Recovered from Cursor
History*. The owner believes it was derived from Cursor histories that have
since been deleted. The underlying sessions were not available here and their
contents have not been independently verified.

Accordingly, this document is a **secondary reconstruction**, not a primary
archive or scientific source. It may guide product design, but it must not be
cited as proof that a particular historical session, artifact, result, or
decision existed. Where exact historical provenance matters, the machine should
record it as unavailable rather than infer it from this summary.

## Ideas retained

The reconstruction contributes five durable design principles:

1. Start with the phenomenon and the practical decision, including the minimum
   evidence required and observations that would change the decision.
2. Maintain one authoritative machine-readable spine; projections, summaries,
   navigation, exploration, and archives must not become competing authorities.
3. Separate documented facts, source claims, project interpretations,
   reasonable inferences, and unresolved questions.
4. Make review a measurement against declared checks for coverage, provenance,
   consistency, disconfirmation, reproducibility, and decision readiness.
5. Iterate reversibly: checkpoint, inspect, change one coherent unit, validate,
   compare against the prior model, and preserve the decision trail.

These ideas complement—not replace—the existing claim hierarchy, competing
hypotheses, protected protocols, immutable evidence, validation tags, rigor
audit, and hash-chained ledger.

## Product changes derived from it

- Inquiries record the decision they are meant to support, minimum evidence,
  decision-change criteria, and decision owner.
- Claims explicitly distinguish epistemic layer from project disposition and
  may record confidence, source references, dependencies, conflicts,
  falsification references, and review metadata.
- The rigor audit checks decision readiness, claim-reference integrity,
  dependency cycles, unsupported sourced/factual claims, and unresolved
  accepted conflicts.
- Deterministic syntheses expose these fields instead of allowing prose to blur
  them.

## Ideas not copied literally

- Confidence is optional and is never treated as a calibrated probability
  unless a separate method establishes calibration.
- Hypothesis workflow remains separate from claim disposition; a generated
  hypothesis is not smuggled into the authoritative claim spine.
- The numeric 0–5 maturity example is not adopted as a universal score. The
  existing audit emits evidence-backed findings and capability ceilings; future
  domain rubrics may add scores only when their anchors are operationally
  defined.
- Repository breadth/depth sweeps and autonomous repair queues remain useful
  engineering playbooks, but are not promoted to scientific evidence objects.
